So my work is on LAMP, which stands for LLM to ASP Modeling Protocol. I originally started this as a way to compare LLM reasoning capabilities on a hard symbolic task, but LAMP ended up performing surprisingly well in its own right. It tackles ASP rule induction, going from example input facts and an expected answer set back to the rules that produced them. People usually find that pretty hard to do by hand. But local LLMs like gpt-oss have gotten good enough to do it almost as well as ILASP, or Inductive Learning of Answer Set Programs, the leading inductive logic programming system. They even succeed sometimes where ILASP times out. It's similar to how LLMs have improved at code generation in general. You can watch the thinking tokens and see the model deducing the results step by step. Since these models weren't explicitly trained on ASP, they're likely picking up some natural heuristics.

LAMP itself is an iterative prompt system designed to get the best results out of the three LLMs I tested. The LLM proposes rules, Clingo verifies them, and any mismatch gets fed back into the next prompt. I tested it on 120 synthetically generated benchmarks of varying complexity, varying the number of rules, rule size, and count of positive and negative body literals. Two of the three LLMs hit over 98 percent accuracy, basically matching ILASP at 97.5 percent. ILASP is fast on average because it uses a conflict driven exhaustive search over an ASP meta encoding, but it timed out after 7 minutes on three benchmarks that both strong LLMs solved. The rules coming out of both the LLMs and ILASP were significantly smaller and all became stratified compared to the original synthetic programs. A natural next step I'm exploring is a hybrid system, combining ILASP's exhaustive search and an agentic LLM loop, to get the best overall results from both on more realistic benchmarks, such as ASPBench.
